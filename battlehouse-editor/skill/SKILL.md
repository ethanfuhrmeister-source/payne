# BattleHouse Live story editing guide

> **Status: STARTER.** Built from standard reality-TV story-producing practice, not yet from
> finished BattleHouse episodes (Season 1 premieres Oct 4, 2026). Lines marked `[VERIFY]` are
> house-style choices to confirm with the showrunner/lead editor and then lock in.

## The show
BattleHouse Live: 30 creators in one mansion competing for $100,000, hosted by Brandon Gomes
and Coach K, streamed live on YouTube, TikTok and Kick. Viewers vote and decide consequences.
Clippers handle short-form. **Our job is the story edit**: turning hours of footage into an
episode that plays like a reality show, not a stream highlight reel.

## Your role: story producer
1. **Read everything first.** Identify every storyline before picking a single soundbite.
2. **Find the A, B and C stories.**
   - **A story** – the main conflict that drives the episode (usually tied to the vote,
     elimination or challenge). It gets the most time and the act-outs.
   - **B story** – a second relationship/conflict (alliance forming, a feud, a romance).
   - **C story** – lighter runner (comedy, a house prank, someone's personal goal).
   Interleave them; don't play one story start to finish.
3. **Every storyline needs an arc**: setup → complication → escalation → turn → payoff
   (or cliffhanger if it continues next episode).
4. **Use the story bible** (cast and running storylines – `bible.md`, provided alongside this
   guide or in the conversation). Cast names are their social media
   handles, and transcripts spell them the way they sound ("Tommy Two Coats" for Tommy2Coats,
   "Kaycee" for Kayceewins) - match them using the bible's "Sounds like" column and always
   write the bible name in your output. Pay off threads from earlier episodes,
   keep names and relationships consistent, and flag new storylines.

## Episode structure  `[VERIFY length and act count]`
- **Cold open (30–90 s):** the most dramatic tease from later in the episode, cut short
  before the payoff. Can be out of chronological order — that's normal.
- **Act 1:** set up stakes for the day (challenge, vote, consequence), introduce A and B stories.
- **Acts 2–3:** escalate. Each act ends on an **act-out**: an unresolved moment, a line that
  raises a question, a shocked reaction.
- **Final act:** the payoff — vote result / elimination / challenge result — then a short
  **tag**: aftermath and a tease of what's next.

## Soundbite selection
- Prefer lines that **state feelings and stakes** ("I don't trust her anymore") over logistics.
- A great sequence: **line → reaction → line**. Note reaction shots even with no dialogue.
- **Host lines** (Brandon Gomes / Coach K) are the narrators: use them to set stakes and
  announce results instead of writing VO.
- **Viewer votes are the twist engine** – make the audience's power felt (votes landing,
  creators reacting to chat).
- Trim inside sentences only where the meaning stays true. **Never stitch words to make
  someone say something they didn't mean** – mark any edit that changes meaning.
- Cover gaps with b-roll and flag what's missing (e.g. "need wide of the pool for challenge
  setup", "no clear reaction from Jess – check other angle").

## Pacing & tone  `[VERIFY]`
- Average soundbite 3–12 s; a beat is usually 20–90 s.
- Comedy moments get music stings; tension gets drones and silence before reveals.
- Don't explain what the audience just saw. Let reactions land.

## Output expectations
For each act: beats in order. For each beat: its purpose (what it does for the story), the
shots in order with exact source + timecodes, and notes for music, SFX, b-roll and graphics.
Finish with coverage gaps and bible updates (new storylines, changed relationships).
